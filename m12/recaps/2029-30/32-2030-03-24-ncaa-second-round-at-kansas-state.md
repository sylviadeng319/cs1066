A season to remember for Harvard women's basketball came to an end on Sunday at Bramlage Coliseum in Manhattan, Kan., where No. 2 seed Kansas State beat the No. 10 seed Crimson, 60-50, in the NCAA Tournament Second Round.

Harvard (28-4) actually shot better than the Wildcats, 39.2% to 34.8%, but Kansas State turned 14 steals and 15 offensive rebounds into 15 more shot attempts than the Crimson. Harvard committed 20 turnovers, fell behind 22-12 in the first quarter, and trailed 37-20 at halftime. The Crimson never stopped fighting, though, and closed the game with 14 unanswered points.

The loss ends the collegiate careers of seniors Niamh Hollis, Isla Marchetti, Brielle Mendoza, and Lauren Wakefield. This year's Crimson won its first 17 games, captured the Ivy League Tournament, and earned the program's first NCAA Tournament win since 1998.

"It hurts right now, and it should, because this group gave everything it had. Kansas State is a great team, and they made it hard on us from the start," said Carrie Moore, The Kathy Delaney-Smith Head Coach for Harvard Women's Basketball. "But I told our players that this game doesn't define them. They won a championship, they won in this tournament, and our seniors have set a standard for everyone who comes after them."

## How it Happened

Kansas State scored first, and first-year Esme Ellison got Harvard on the board at the free throw line. The Wildcats controlled the opening quarter and led 22-12 after 10 minutes.

Kansas State scored the first 11 points of the second quarter to go up 33-12. Harvard found some offense late in the half but went to the locker room down 37-20.

The Crimson battled the Wildcats evenly in the third quarter, with each team scoring 11 points, and Harvard trailed 48-31 entering the fourth.

Kansas State pushed its lead to 60-36 with a 12-2 run early in the final frame. Harvard refused to go quietly. The Crimson scored the game's last 14 points to cut into the margin, outscoring the Wildcats 19-12 in the fourth quarter, but the deficit was too much to overcome.

## Harvard Highlights

- Harvard's season ends in the NCAA Tournament Second Round with a 28-4 record, an Ivy League Tournament championship, and an NCAA Tournament First Round win.
- Junior Thea Sutherland led Harvard with nine points (4-of-8 FG, 1-of-1 3-PT) and five rebounds.
- First-year Jordan Delgado added nine points on 4-of-6 shooting off the bench.
- Senior Isla Marchetti scored eight points with three steals, and first-year Esme Ellison added eight points.
- Senior Brielle Mendoza finished her career with six points, a team-high nine rebounds, and two blocks.
- Senior Niamh Hollis scored six points on 3-of-5 shooting and grabbed four rebounds in her final game.
- Kansas State finished with 14 steals and 15 offensive rebounds and attempted 66 shots to Harvard's 51.
