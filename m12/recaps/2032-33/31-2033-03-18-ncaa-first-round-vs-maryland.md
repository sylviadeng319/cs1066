Harvard women's basketball's first trip to the NCAA Tournament since 2029-30 ended sooner than the Crimson hoped. No. 8 seed Maryland pulled away in the third quarter for a 67-50 win over No. 9 seed Harvard in the First Round on Friday at Carver-Hawkeye Arena in Iowa City, Iowa.

The Crimson (27-4) hung within four at halftime and was within three early in the third quarter, but the Terrapins answered with 14 straight points and never looked back. Maryland shot 56.9% from the field, while Harvard was held to 32.7% despite forcing 18 turnovers and recording 13 steals.

The loss closes a season that included a 24-game winning streak, a first-place Ivy League finish, and an Ivy League Tournament title. It also ends the collegiate careers of seniors Tiana Adeyemi, Jordan Delgado, Esme Ellison, and Chloe Ferreira.

"It hurts right now, and it should, because this group cared so much. But I don't want one tough game to take anything away from what they built. Our seniors brought this program back to the NCAA Tournament, and the standard they set will be here long after they're gone," said Carrie Moore, The Kathy Delaney-Smith Head Coach for Harvard Women's Basketball.

## How it Happened

Adeyemi scored the game's first basket on a floater, but Maryland answered with a 15-6 run to go up 15-8. Harvard closed the first quarter strong and trailed just 15-12 after 10 minutes.

The Terrapins opened the second quarter with an 11-2 run to build a 26-14 lead. The Crimson fought back with a 12-4 burst, capped by an Adeyemi 3-pointer, to pull within 32-28 at halftime.

Harvard kept coming early in the third. Jasmine Danforth, Ferreira, and Ellison keyed a 9-0 run that cut the deficit to 42-39. But Maryland responded with a 14-0 run midway through the quarter, and the Crimson trailed 56-39 entering the fourth. Harvard matched the Terrapins 11-11 in the final period but could not string together the stops it needed to mount a comeback.

## Harvard Highlights

- Harvard's season ends in the NCAA Tournament First Round with a 27-4 record, an Ivy League Tournament championship, and a first-place finish in the Ivy League standings.
- Senior Jordan Delgado led Harvard with 11 points (4-of-9 FG, 2-of-6 3-PT) and a team-high seven assists in her final collegiate game.
- Senior Chloe Ferreira hit three 3-pointers for nine points and added four steals.
- Junior Jasmine Danforth and senior Esme Ellison each scored eight points, and Ellison grabbed eight rebounds.
- Senior Tiana Adeyemi scored seven points off the bench and had three steals.
- Harvard forced 18 turnovers and recorded 13 steals.
