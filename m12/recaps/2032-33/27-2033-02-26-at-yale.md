One night after its 24-game winning streak ended at Brown, Harvard women's basketball made sure the slide stopped at one. The Crimson (25-2, 12-1 Ivy) poured in 31 points in the first quarter and never trailed in a 79-61 win over Yale on Saturday at John J. Lee Amphitheater.

The victory completed a season sweep of the Bulldogs, who beat Harvard in New Haven a year ago, and kept the Crimson atop the Ivy League standings with one regular-season game left. Harvard committed just seven turnovers, forced 16, and made 11 of its 13 free throws.

"Last night stung, and I wanted to see how we'd respond. We came out with a lot of energy and took care of the basketball, and that's a mature performance from this group on the second night of a road trip," said head coach Carrie Moore.

## How it Happened

Harvard scored first and never looked back. The Crimson's offense was humming from the opening tip, and Harvard built a 31-16 lead after one quarter, its most points in an opening period in Ivy League play this season.

Yale found a rhythm in the second quarter, using a 14-4 run to pull within 42-33. Harvard steadied itself and went into halftime ahead 46-33.

The Crimson put the game away in the third. Senior Tiana Adeyemi, Chloe Ferreira, and Jordan Delgado keyed a 10-0 run that stretched the lead to 59-38, and Harvard carried a 61-43 advantage into the fourth quarter.

The lead reached 25 in the fourth, and Carrie Moore emptied her bench, with all 16 players on the roster seeing action. The bench finished with 24 points.

## Harvard Highlights

- With the win, Harvard improves to 25-2 overall and 12-1 in Ivy League play.
- Harvard swept the season series with Yale, following a 76-47 win at Lavietes Pavilion on Jan. 29.
- Senior Jordan Delgado led Harvard with 20 points (8-of-15 FG, 2-of-5 3-PT, 2-of-2 FT) and three assists.
- Senior Tiana Adeyemi had a season-high 13 points (6-of-10 FG) off the bench to go with five rebounds and a team-high five assists.
- Junior Jasmine Danforth scored 13 points (5-of-8 FG, 1-of-2 3-PT, 2-of-2 FT) and blocked two shots.
- Esme Ellison (10) and Chloe Ferreira (10) also reached double figures, giving Harvard five double-digit scorers.
- Harvard assisted on 19 of its 31 field goals and turned the ball over just seven times.
